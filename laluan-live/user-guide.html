User Guide

For LaluanLive v0.3 · Last updated: November 21, 2026

LaluanLive is a community-powered way to check live status, weather and rider reports for Klang Valley LRT and MRT lines, brought to you by Foooverse.

Getting Started

No sign-up is required. LaluanLive covers all six lines: Kelana Jaya, Ampang, Sri Petaling, Kajang, Putrajaya and Shah Alam.

The first time you open it, the board asks Which lines do you ride? Tap the lines you use to follow them, then tap Done, or tap Show all lines to skip. Followed lines come first on the board and shape what the headline talks about. They're saved on your phone only. You can change them any time from the menu (Your lines) or with the star on a line's page.

Tip: add LaluanLive to your phone's Home Screen so it opens like an app. On iPhone this is also what lets you get alerts (see "Alerts" below).

The Board

The board is built to answer three questions at a glance: is my line OK, is the weather affecting me, and what should I do? From top to bottom:

  1. Header: the logo, a Live · updated 9:58 am pill, and the menu button. If the last refresh failed, the pill turns amber and says Offline · last 9:58 am, and the board keeps showing what it last knew.
  2. Headline: one or two lines that sum up the situation, with a small label above it such as "YOUR LINES · THIS MORNING 8:02 AM". For example:
    • "All your lines are running."
    • "KJ stopped at Dang Wangi. Rain over KL."
    • "Your lines are running. Rain till 1 am."
    • "Weather data delayed. Rail is live." or "Rapid KL notices delayed. Rider reports are live." when a source has gone quiet, so a quiet board is never mistaken for "all clear".
  3. Live city map: see "The Live City Map" below.
  4. Map key: numbered chips that match the numbered pins on the map, for example "1 · KJ stopped at Dang Wangi" or "2 · Heavy rain · till 1 am". Tap a chip that names a line to open it, or a weather chip to open the weather page.
  5. Line list: every line with its code, name and status. If it's raining now on part of a line, the row also shows where, for example ☂ Masjid Jamek – KL Sentral (see "Raining Now"). Tap a row to open the line.
  6. Action button: when something needs your attention, a button suggests a next step, such as Try Kajang from Pasar Seni → (the nearest interchange to a line that's running), See KJ details → or See rain detail →. It's hidden when all is well.
  7. Report button: the round green button opens the report sheet.

Statuses

  • Normal: no active problems reported
  • Delayed: slower trains or longer waits
  • Disrupted: a serious problem on part of the line, reported by Rapid KL or by several riders
  • Stopped: no trains on part of the line. Rapid KL says service is suspended, or several riders report a breakdown. (This used to be called "Halted".)

Each status has its own icon shape as well as a colour, so it reads without relying on colour.

When a line's status changes while the app is open, its row is outlined once and says Just changed for about a minute and a half. If it's one of your followed lines, you also get a short message at the top of the screen, and Android phones vibrate.

The menu

The menu button (top right) opens Your lines (tap to follow or unfollow), Weather, Alerts, How this works and Refresh now. At the bottom it shows roughly how many people are using LaluanLive right now. That's a rough indicator of activity, not an exact count.

The Live City Map

The board shows a small 3D model of the Klang Valley rail network, drawn from real station positions and track alignments, with rivers, buildings and landmarks such as the Petronas Twin Towers, Merdeka 118, Exchange 106, Merdeka Square, Batu Caves, Thean Hou Temple and Masjid Putra. It focuses on what's happening:

  • Problems on a line are marked with numbered pins, and the affected stretch keeps its line colour while the rest of the city fades back.
  • Official weather warning areas show as soft circles, and clouds and rain are drawn where riders say it's raining now.
  • The sky follows the time of day in Kuala Lumpur: sunrise and sunset light, the sun by day, and the moon, stars and lit windows at night. The forecast adds clouds, and the sun only shows when no rain is expected.

Moving around the map

  • Phone: one finger scrolls the page as usual. Use two fingers to turn and pinch to zoom. You can also use the +, − and turn buttons on the map.
  • Computer: drag to turn and tilt, scroll to zoom.
  • Hover over or tap a landmark or tall tower to see its name.

Reading the sky

The weather on the map tells you different things depending on how it's drawn. Rain only falls where riders say it's raining now. A forecast or an official warning only adds clouds or an outline, never falling rain.

Through the day

The light follows the time in Kuala Lumpur, so the map also tells you at a glance whether it's dawn, day, dusk or night.

These pictures show the flat version of the map (LaluanLive v0.3). The app shows the same scenes in 3D where your phone or browser can draw it.

On a phone the map draws in stages: the ground and tracks first, then buildings, landmarks and trees, then clouds and rain. You may see Loading map… for a moment. If your browser can't draw 3D graphics, a flat version of the same map is shown instead. The map is a picture of the situation, not a navigation map: positions are approximate.

A Line's Page

Tap a line on the board to open its page. Each line has its own link, and Back returns you to the board. At the top, next to the line's name, are the star (follow this line, which puts it first on the board) and the bell (alerts for this line). Below that:

  1. Status: the line's current status, the reason, and where it came from (Rapid KL or rider reports).
  2. Line strip: a short diagram of the stations around the problem, with the problem station marked. If it's raining now on part of the line, a ☂ RAINING NOW band covers just that stretch, with who reported it (for example "3 riders"). Tap the band for the weather page.
  3. Same here and Running fine: Same here · N says you're seeing the same problem as the latest rider report (tap again to withdraw). If there's no rider report to agree with, it opens the report sheet with the problem already picked. Running fine tells other riders the line looks normal to you.
  4. Weather warning: while MetMalaysia has a warning covering stations on this line, a card shows the level, the area, until when, how many of this line's stations are in it, a link to MetMalaysia and Rain detail.
  5. Official updates (Rapid KL): notices from Rapid KL, the ones still active first. If there are none, you'll see "No Rapid KL notice for this line right now".
  6. Rider reports (Unverified): reports from other riders, each with an Agree button.
  7. Stations: the line drawn as a rail, with interchanges marked, stations with reports highlighted, ☂ on stations where it's raining now, and short tags such as "Wet exit" or "2 reports". Tap a station to see only that station's updates, then Station view → for a picture of that station (street, concourse and platform) with what's been reported there.

When the line has a problem, Other ways to go → leads to alternatives (see "Finding Another Way").

Raining Now

The ☂ on stations, on the line strip and on board rows means it's actually raining there now, as reported by riders. Forecasts and official warnings never put a ☂ on a station: they say rain is expected, and they appear as text instead.

  • A station shows ☂ when 2 or more different riders tap Raining here at that station or the next station either way on the same line, within 20 minutes of each other.
  • It clears 30 minutes after the last of those reports.
  • Neighbouring rainy stations join into one stretch, such as "Masjid Jamek – KL Sentral". On the board, a line with more than one stretch shows the one nearest your saved stations, plus "+1".
  • No ☂ doesn't mean dry. It means no one has reported rain there. The line page says Rain from rider reports as a reminder.
  • If the rain data can't be refreshed, every ☂ is hidden and you'll see Weather data delayed instead.

Rain never changes a line's status, its colour or the order of the list.

Measured rain from JPS (Department of Irrigation and Drainage) rain gauges may be added later, once JPS gives permission. Stations near a gauge would then also show rain, labelled "JPS gauge".

Weather and Flood Warnings

Open Weather from the menu, from a weather chip under the map, or from Rain detail on a warning card.

When MET Malaysia (MetMalaysia) issues a heavy rain, thunderstorm or flood warning for a district with stations, LaluanLive matches it to the stations in that district. The weather page shows it as a water gauge:

  • The title gives the level: Rain level: Yellow, Orange or Red. When MetMalaysia gives no level (often for thunderstorms) it just says MetMalaysia warning.
  • A sentence says what, where and until when, for example "Heavy rain over KL till 1 am", plus the districts.
  • Lines with stations in the warning area sit below the waterline, each with how many stations, which stretch, and the line's current status. Tap one to open that line. Lines with no stations in the area sit above the line.
  • The water shows the warning level, not flood depth.

Other things you may see on the weather page:

  • "No MetMalaysia warning for the Klang Valley right now", when warnings are being checked and there are none.
  • "Warning data delayed. Last checked …", when we haven't heard from MetMalaysia for a while. We never say "no warnings" when we can't be sure.
  • "MET Malaysia's forecast for the Klang Valley. Official warnings aren't connected yet, so no warning level is shown." In this case the page shows the day's forecast (for example "This afternoon: Thunderstorms in places") but no warning level.

A warning is an area warning: it doesn't mean a station is flooded, and it never changes a line's status. Warnings are checked about every 15 minutes, and about every 5 minutes while a warning is active.

Not an official warning service. Information may be delayed. Follow MetMalaysia, JPS/InfoBanjir and NADMA. In an emergency, call 999.

Alerts

Open Alerts from the menu, or tap the bell on a line's page. Alerts arrive even when LaluanLive is closed.

Line alerts

Turn on a line to get a notification when it becomes delayed, disrupted or stopped, and again when it's back to normal. You can follow as many lines as you like.

  • "Kelana Jaya Line: delayed", with "Official notice from Rapid KL" when Rapid KL posted it.
  • "Kelana Jaya Line: disrupted", with "Reported by several riders (not yet confirmed by Rapid KL)" when the change comes from riders.
  • "Kelana Jaya Line back to normal" when the problem is over.

Weather & flood alerts

Turn on Weather & flood to get a notification when a MetMalaysia warning covers stations on the lines you get alerts for, or any station you pin. Use + Pin station to add up to 20 extra stations, such as the one near your home or office. An alert looks like "Orange heavy rain warning · KL" with "Heavy rain till 1 am. Source: MET Malaysia. Not an official warning service."

  • You get one alert per warning. An update to the same warning replaces the earlier notification.
  • Quiet hours · 11 pm – 6 am is on by default. During those hours, yellow and orange warnings wait until 6 am, and are only sent then if the warning is still running. Red warnings always come through.
  • Rider "Raining here" reports don't send alerts.

On iPhone or iPad

iPhone only sends alerts from websites added to the Home Screen (iOS 16.4 or later). If you try to turn on alerts in Safari, the app shows you these steps:

  1. In Safari, tap Share.
  2. Choose Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  3. Open LaluanLive from your Home Screen and go to Alerts (or tap the bell on your line).
  4. When asked, tap Allow.

On Android or a computer

Turn on an alert and allow notifications when your browser asks. That's it.

Alerts can arrive up to about 5 minutes after a change. They follow your phone's own notification, sound and Focus settings. An alert that couldn't be delivered within 30 minutes (for example because your phone was off) is dropped rather than shown late. Tap an alert to open the app.

Turning alerts off

Switch off each line, and the Weather & flood switch, in Alerts, or tap the bell again on a line's page. Once nothing is on, your alert subscription is deleted. You can also turn off notifications for LaluanLive in your phone's settings at any time.

Reporting a Problem

Tap the round green Report button. The report sheet takes three taps and no typing:

  1. Line: already filled in when you start from a line's page.
  2. Station (optional, except for rain): tap Change to pick one, or Near me to pick the nearest station within 3 km. Near me asks for your location once and uses it on your phone only. Only the chosen station is sent, and if you say no, nothing changes.
  3. What you see: Delayed, Stopped, Crowded, Raining here, Wet / flooded exit, Aircond / lights, or Something else.

Tap Send report. Your report shows as "Rider report · Unverified" at the station you chose.

  • Raining here needs a station. It feeds the ☂ indicators (see "Raining Now") and is deleted 24 hours after you send it.
  • Wet / flooded exit and Aircond / lights describe a station, not the trains, so they show on the station but never change a line's status.

To keep things fair: one report per line every 5 minutes, and up to 6 reports an hour. If you hit a limit, the app tells you. A report closes by itself once the line has been quiet for 30 minutes, or sooner when three different riders say the line is running fine. Nobody can flag or remove it, and reporting is never blocked for your device.

Agreeing with a Report

If another rider's report matches what you're seeing, tap Agree on it, or Same here on the line's page. You can't agree with your own report or with one that's already resolved, and you can withdraw your agreement later. Agreeing shows other riders how many people are noticing the same thing, but it doesn't change a line's status on its own.

Confirming a Line Is Running Fine

On a line's page, tap Running fine to let other riders know things seem normal. It's one tap, and you can confirm each line once an hour. Confirmations don't appear in the feed. When three different riders confirm, an open rider report on that line can close early, unless a Rapid KL notice for the line is still open. Confirmations never change a line's status directly.

Finding Another Way

When a line has a problem, tap Other ways to go → on its page (or the action button on the board). The page lists options from the affected station:

  • Rapid KL says: Rapid KL's own alternative, such as replacement buses, when the notice includes one. Most notices only reach LaluanLive as a short title, so this is often missing. Use "View official notice" for full details.
  • Other lines from the nearest interchanges, best first. A line that's running is marked BEST RIGHT NOW, with Open in Google Maps for directions to the interchange.
  • Feeder bus or e-hailing: live positions of MRT feeder buses near the station (where there are any, refreshed every 30 seconds), Open in Google Maps at the station, and Book a ride, which opens Grab's website without filling in a pickup.
  • If heavy rain is on the way, a note says walks may be wet.
  • Rather wait? Turn on the line's alert to be told when it's moving again.

Suggestions come from the line map and live status, not from Rapid KL, so check signs at the station. LaluanLive doesn't know where you are or where you're going, and doesn't plan routes itself. The links hand you over to other services, which have their own terms and privacy policies.

Reading an Update

Every update shows where it came from, plus a clock time and how long ago, for example "8:02 am · 12 min ago":

  • Official · Rapid KL: a notice from Rapid KL's own service announcements.
  • Rider report · Unverified: a report from another rider. It's labelled Unverified because Rapid KL hasn't confirmed it.

Official notices come from the announcements Rapid KL publishes on its website, which LaluanLive checks every 10 minutes at peak times (5–10 am and 4–9 pm), every 30 minutes at other times and every 2 hours overnight. Each one gets a short English title, such as "Train delay: Kelana Jaya Line · Gombak". A notice about several lines shows on each of them, and a named station is marked on the line's page. Tap View official notice for Rapid KL's full announcement in Malay and English. Notices can lag behind what's happening on the platform. Rapid KL's general updates also appear, titled "Update". They're for information only and never change a line's status or send alerts.

If LaluanLive can't reach Rapid KL's announcements for a while, the board says notices may be delayed, so a quiet board never pretends everything is fine.

How a Line's Status Is Decided

Rider reports from the last hour are combined to work out a line's status:

  • Each device counts once, using its most serious report.
  • A breakdown ("Stopped") or a delay counts for more than crowding or "Something else". One breakdown or delay report is enough to mark a line Delayed; a single crowding report is not. Disrupted takes more, or more serious, reports, and Stopped needs reports from several different riders.
  • Raining here, Wet / flooded exit and Aircond / lights never count towards a line's status.
  • Rapid KL notices set a minimum: the line is at least as bad as the notice says. The final status is whichever is worse.
  • Weather warnings, Agree, Same here and Running fine never change the status directly.

When an Issue Closes

  • Rapid KL says service is back. The earlier open items for that line close.
  • Quiet for 30 minutes. If a line sees no new activity for 30 minutes, its open items close. Official notices with a repair target more than 6 hours after they were published stay open until Rapid KL says it's over or replaces them.
  • Riders say it's running fine. When three different riders confirm, an open rider report can close early, unless a Rapid KL notice for that line is still open.

A repair date never closes anything by itself. If it passes with no update, the notice is marked "date passed, awaiting update". Closed items are deleted 48 hours later.

On a Computer

On a wide screen (about 1024 pixels and up) the board has two columns: the headline, the larger city map and its key on the left, and a green What to do card, the line list and the weather summary on the right. A line's page keeps the side-by-side view of lines, updates and details. Weather, Alerts and Other ways open in a centred column.

Refreshing

Pull down on the screen, or use Refresh now in the menu. While the app is open it also refreshes by itself every minute. What you see is usually less than a minute old and at most a couple of minutes, because the data is cached briefly to keep the app fast.

Light and Dark

The app follows the time of day in Kuala Lumpur: the light look by day, warm light around sunrise and sunset, and the dark look at night. There's no manual switch.

About Your Data

  • No account or personal information is required. Your device gets a random anonymous ID, kept in a cookie and local storage, used to count each device once and to apply the limits above.
  • Your followed lines, alert choices and pinned stations are saved on your phone. If you turn on alerts, a copy of your alert choices is kept with your browser's push address so we can send them.
  • Near me uses your location on your phone only. Your location is never sent to us or stored.
  • Reports are deleted 48 hours after they're resolved. "Raining here" reports are deleted 24 hours after they're sent.

For full details, see our Privacy Policy. The How this works page in the app (in the menu) also explains where updates come from, what each status means and what data is kept.
